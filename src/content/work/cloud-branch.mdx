## The hard part was not the UI

The bank could not simply replace its core systems. We had to simplify the employee experience while keeping existing checks, approvals and auditability intact.

## The problem

A customer visit could require a branch employee to move between multiple applications, each with its own login, interaction patterns, transaction logic and status terminology.

A representative fund-transfer journey spanned **3 systems and 25+ clicks**, with hand-offs between people and tools. The goal was to reduce switching without removing the controls branch operations depend on.

## Three decisions shaped the work

<DecisionBlock
  n="03"
  decision="Reuse the operating logic, not just the UI"
  problem="Transfers, deposits and withdrawals each need different details, and a shared component library alone would not stop them behaving differently."
  evidence="Across those services the service-specific details changed, but much of the underlying journey stayed the same."
  why="Reusable logic mattered more than making every screen look identical."
  consequence="A shared pattern — identify, capture, review, route and resolve — with each service adding its own rules, documents and approval levels on top."
  result="A new service inherits the operating logic rather than renegotiating its shape."
/>

## Takeaway

Different banking services did not need identical screens. They needed a shared operating logic that staff could learn once and reuse.
